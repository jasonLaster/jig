# Lee stool: fit, rounded edges and thumb access

These are proposed workshop starting points. Verify them with the intended users and a finished sample made from the actual wood.

| Setting                | Starting point                                    | Purpose                                                |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Letter top roundover   | 1/16 inch (1.59 mm)                               | Soften the insert edge touched during lifting          |
| Pocket rim roundover   | 1/16 inch (1.59 mm), constrained by nearby curves | Soften the facing top edge and lifting well            |
| Finished letter fit    | 0.8–1.2 mm per side                               | Free removal without a large visible straight-wall gap |
| Easier child lifting   | 1.2–1.5 mm per side                               | More room when fit or finish is inconsistent           |
| Child thumb well       | 3/8–1/2-inch radius (9.5–12.7 mm)                 | 19–25 mm nominal circular diameter                     |
| Adult thumb well       | 1/2–5/8-inch radius (12.7–15.9 mm)                | 25–32 mm nominal circular diameter                     |
| Shared adult/child use | Try 1/2-inch radius                               | An accessible opening for both; test the actual thumbs |

The selected 0.0379-inch letter clearance is about 0.96 mm per side, already in the ordinary starting range. The selected 3/8-inch well radius remains the default. Thumb sizes vary with age and individual anatomy: measure the pad that will do the lifting. A useful proposed rule is **nominal well diameter = thumb-pad width + 2–4 mm**, then divide by two for the radius. This is a shallow lifting recess; assess access and withdrawal through the full depth, rather than treating its diameter as a population guarantee. [NIST's historical AnthroKids measurement procedures](https://math.nist.gov/~SRessler/anthrokids/data1977/descrip.htm) define thumb-joint diameter separately from hand breadth and thumb length. That historical measurement definition does not establish the recommended well sizes above.

The circular portion of the modeled well has diameter 2r. Tangent entry curves widen its mouth and remove sharp plan corners; the rim roundover opens its top further. The model constrains well size to the available straight letter edge, and disables oversized presets. Wider letters can provide a larger well. All radii describe the effective shared geometry used by the preview, STLs and layout.

Roundovers make the upper edge comfortable and improve the opening at the surface. They do not replace clearance along the straight walls. Nominal surface separation along an ordinary straight letter edge is approximately **wall clearance + letter roundover + pocket rim roundover**; the fit below the rounded bands is still the wall clearance.

For a machined gap g, cured finish consumes approximately the letter-side film thickness plus the pocket-side film thickness on each wall. Leave additional allowance for machining error and differential seasonal movement. [USDA Forest Products Laboratory, Wood Handbook chapter 13 (2021)](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62261) explains that seasonal moisture changes affect wood dimensions and that protective coatings retard those changes without preventing them. Different species and grain directions can move differently, so there is no universal guaranteed wood fit.

Before cutting the complete top, make one pocket/letter/well coupon. Use the planned grain directions, sanding and finish; let the finish cure. Try the ordinary 0.8–1.2 mm gap, then enlarge it if removal needs force. Test with the adult and child who will use the stool, including lifting, tilted withdrawal and re-insertion. A loose insert should lift without wedging. The 3/4-inch pocket depth gives substantial access depth; check reach as well as thumb width.
